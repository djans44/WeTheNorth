Rules

The league

{{ s.team_count }} teams on Yahoo Fantasy. Two quarterbacks, superflex, half PPR.

Starters are QB, two WR, two RB, a TE, a W/R/T and a Q/W/R/T, plus a defence. Four bench spots and two IR. There is no kicker slot.

The draft is 13 rounds, snake order.

The season

Fourteen weeks of regular season, then three weeks of playoffs.

Each manager plays three opponents twice and the other eight once. The doubled-up pairings favour whoever you have faced least often, so the schedule evens out over the years rather than within one season.

  • Weeks 1 to 11 are a full round robin — everyone plays everyone once.
  • Week 10 is rivalry week. Every manager plays their rival.
  • Weeks 12 to 14 are the rematches.
  • No pair ever meets in consecutive weeks.

Playoffs

Six teams make the championship bracket.

  • The top two seeds get a first-round bye.
  • Third and fourth qualify on record.
  • The last two spots go to the two remaining teams with the most points for, whatever their record.

The bracket has been the same every year:

WeekChampionship bracket Everyone else
15 Two quarterfinals, two byes Four consolation games
16 Two semifinals Fifth place, eleventh place, two consolation
17 Championship, third place Seventh place, ninth place

Your final position from first to twelfth is settled by those named placement games, not by record. Win the third-place game and you finished third; lose it and you finished fourth.

Keepers

You may keep up to {{ s.keeper_count }} players, drawn from your end-of-season roster — not from who drafted them. Trade for a player in November and he is yours to keep.

What a keeper costs

Keeping a player costs you that round in the draft. The price is his original draft round, or round 13 if he was picked up off waivers or free agency at any point that season.

  • A trade carries the price with the player, unchanged. Drafted then traded keeps the draft round; picked up off waivers then traded stays 13.
  • Drafted, dropped, then added back by the same team is 13. You gave him up.

How long you can keep him

Four seasons at most: one year as a plain keeper, then up to three more under contract.

  • Year one costs his original round. No commitment.
  • Year two requires a contract. You choose one year or three.
  • Once a contract expires the player is ineligible for one year, after which he resets to being an ordinary non-keeper.

When two keepers want the same round

No two of your keepers may cost the same round. The one who arrived later moves up to the next free, more expensive round. A player already under contract never moves, so the newcomer is the one that shifts.

Which means two round-one keepers are impossible. There is nowhere earlier to go.

Contracts

At the start of a player's second keeper year you sign him for one year or for three.

  • One year — he costs his original round again, and is then ineligible the following season.
  • Three years — year one at his original round, then years two and three at whichever is cheaper: his original round, or his ADP round plus two. That price is locked when you sign and never moves again, however he performs.

A contract is an obligation, not an option.

If you are holding a contracted player when keeper season comes round, you must keep him, and he fills one of your {{ s.keeper_count }} slots.

Contracts follow the player, not the manager. Trade for a contracted player and you inherit the terms exactly as they stand. The commissioner will block any trade that would leave a manager holding a fourth contracted player.

Voiding a contract

The only way out of a contract.

  • The slot frees up immediately — you may still keep {{ s.keeper_count }} players that year.
  • You are forced to draft a defence at the contract's round plus three, capped at round 13.
  • You may void more than one contract in a season. Each carries its own forced defence pick.

The wasted roster spot is the price. That is the whole point of it.

Voids must be declared during the first keeper phase, and have their own submit button. A void you tick but never submit is discarded when that phase resolves, and the contract stands.

Choosing your keepers

Selection runs in three phases, one keeper each, each with its own submission window.

  • Contracts fill the earliest phases automatically, longest remaining contract first. What round they cost has nothing to do with the order.
  • Your free choices fill whatever phases are left.
  • You can plan all three phases in advance. A plan submits itself when its window closes.
  • Miss a window with no contract to fill the slot and you forfeit that keeper.

Contracts and forfeits are approved automatically. Anything you chose yourself is reviewed. If a submission is rejected the phase reopens and your plan survives, so you can adjust and send it again.

You submit only for yourself. Admins can submit on anyone's behalf.

Draft order and the board

The lottery

Ballots are handed out by where you finished last season, in tiers:

FinishedBallots
1st to 3rd1
4th to 6th2
7th to 12th3

Twenty-seven ballots in the hat. A bottom-six finisher is three times as likely to be drawn as a top-three finisher.

Choosing your slot

The lottery does not set the draft order. It sets the order in which you choose your draft slot. Everyone picks their slot in one live session, in the order drawn.

Slots are chosen before keeper selection, so you know where you are drafting from while you decide who to keep.

The board

Snake order. Pick numbers skip over cells already spent on keepers, so the numbers count only the picks you actually make. Forced defence picks from a void count as taken too, and keepers bump around them.

Rivalries

Every manager has exactly one rival each season. Rivalries are mutual, and nobody is two managers' rival.

Joining and leaving

When a manager leaves, their team does not dissolve.

  • The incoming manager takes over the vacant team as it stands, including every keeper and every contract on it.
  • Those contracts carry their existing terms. They are obligations for the new manager exactly as they were for the old one, and can only be escaped by voiding.
  • A new manager skips the lottery and chooses their draft slot first, ahead of everyone else.
